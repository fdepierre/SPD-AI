# SPD-AI: Software Product Development with AI in Europe

This repository gathers working documents on how AI agents change the way software products are built, managed and governed, with a European focus: sovereignty of the tool chain and regulation (AI Act, NIS2, Cyber Resilience Act, GDPR).

The documents are written in French for now. They are generated with AI assistance and human direction, then reviewed and revised as the work progresses.

## Documents

- [Le futur du Product Management à l'ère des agents IA](Le%20futur%20du%20Product%20Management%20%C3%A0%20l%27%C3%A8re%20des%20agents%20IA.md): product value, decisions, knowledge transfer, agents, evidence, organisation and the European context.
- [Développer avec l'IA aujourd'hui et demain, souveraineté européenne](D%C3%A9velopper%20avec%20l%27IA%20aujourd%27hui%20et%20demain%2C%20souverainet%C3%A9%20europ%C3%A9enne.md): development architecture, models, hosting, costs, compliance, and scenarios for a new product or a large migration.

## Status

These are drafts for study, not scientific papers, legal opinions or independently validated findings. Important claims are distinguished as documented facts, an actor's statements, hypotheses, recommendations or open questions.

Some references and figures may still need checking against their primary sources or the project's actual circumstances. Each document should state its generation or revision date and status, retain its sources, and make unresolved questions visible.

Use these documents as background for your own analysis, not as established proof or a guarantee of compliance, performance or cost.

## Consistency between documents

The documents are intended to remain consistent in their definitions, assumptions, figures, regulatory references and recommendations. An AI-assisted consistency review should normally be carried out after substantive changes, under human direction.

This is an editorial practice, not a guarantee or a continuously running automated check. AI can help identify contradictions, outdated references and differences in scope, but important changes and unresolved disagreements still require human review.

Where the documents intentionally use different assumptions or reach different conclusions, those differences should be explained rather than hidden. Cross-document review does not replace checking the underlying sources.
